LEGO History

LEGO was founded in 1932 by Ole Kirk Christiansen in Billund, Denmark, originally as a small carpentry workshop. The name LEGO comes from the Danish phrase "leg godt," meaning "play well," reflecting the company’s mission to create high-quality, imaginative toys that inspire creativity in children.

Over the decades, LEGO grew from a local workshop into a global phenomenon. The company pioneered the concept of interlocking bricks in the late 1940s and gradually expanded its range of products, focusing on building systems that encourage learning, storytelling, and exploration.

LEGO has always emphasized quality, durability, and the power of imagination, setting the foundation for its worldwide popularity. From humble beginnings to becoming a symbol of creativity, LEGO’s history reflects innovation, play, and learning across generations.

Early Toys

In the 1940s and 1950s, LEGO began producing some of its first plastic toys and interlocking bricks, laying the groundwork for the iconic building systems we know today. These early creations sparked imagination and creativity, allowing children to construct simple models and explore new ways of learning through play.

LEGO’s early sets included houses, vehicles, and basic figures that encouraged hands-on experimentation. Each piece was designed with quality and durability in mind, emphasizing the philosophy of “play well” and inspiring generations of builders.
